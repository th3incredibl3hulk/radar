# Frontier Watch — State of the Art

## Overview

No frontier lab shipped a new flagship in this 10-day window (2026-09-07 to 2026-09-17) — the Sept 1-3 pileup (Fable 5.1, Astra, Muse Spark 1.3) has given way to a quieter model-release cadence, and the biggest capability story instead came from DeepSeek: V4.1 Flash (Sept 10) debuts a new "asymmetric" architecture family (552B total params, only 8B/16B active, native multimodal input, deep KV-cache compression), ships MIT-licensed, and beats named flagships like Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on several agentic/coding benchmarks at roughly 20-50x lower API prices. It's the sharpest open-weight cost-efficiency data point tracked to date and resets the "good enough, absurdly cheap" bar for coding-agent and automation workloads.

The two dominant stories this cycle are strategic, not technical. First, Anthropic escalated its distillation dispute with Moonshot AI into an outright fraud accusation: a Sept 10 report claims Moonshot silently routed ~300,000 Kimi user requests to Claude through over 5,300 fraudulent accounts, served Claude's answers back as Kimi's own, and logged the exchanges for training — a serious allegation that, if true, undermines trust in any Kimi benchmark result run during the affected period and raises real data-governance questions for anyone using Kimi in production. Second, xAI's credibility took a genuine hit: Grok 4.7 didn't just slip a fifth time, Musk skipped it outright on Sept 13 and announced Grok 4.8 (2.5T params, new C++ training stack) instead — neither model has a ship date, a spec sheet, or a price as of Sept 17.

Elsewhere, "still unresolved" keeps compounding but with new texture. Gemini 3.5 Pro remains unshipped, but was caught being silently A/B tested inside Google's Antigravity IDE under a fake "Gemini 3.1 Pro" label — Google is gathering real-world signal before committing to a date rather than guessing in isolation. GPT-6 Astra's chain-of-thought monitorability controversy got a substantive system-card update (Sept 9) narrowing OpenAI's own claims rather than resolving them. Artificial Analysis bumped its Intelligence Index to v4.3 within days of the v4.2 overhaul, tying Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra for #1 under the new scale. Anthropic's IPO story moved from investor rumor to company-sourced substance: Nasdaq is now the confirmed venue, and Anthropic told investors it expects a second straight "profitable" (adjusted) quarter on $65B annualized revenue as of July — a claim already drawing skepticism over which costs the adjustment excludes. Hyperscaler capex-vs-cash-flow divergence keeps widening (UBS: $4.1T combined AI infrastructure spend 2026-2028). Meta, Mistral, and Qwen were all confirmed quiet this cycle via individual lab sweeps — a genuine lull, not a search gap.

## Capability Frontiers (executive view)

### Reasoning & Test-Time Compute
Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra are now tied for #1 on AA Intelligence Index v4.3 (53, rescaled), with Opus 5 close behind at 51 — the tightest top-of-leaderboard gap tracked yet, and a reminder that the absolute score isn't comparable across index versions (two version bumps in two weeks). Reasoning-effort dials remain standard product surface across all labs.

### Agents & Long-Horizon Tasks
No new long-horizon demo this cycle to match last cycle's Claude/Prove2Me Fermat's Last Theorem proof, but DeepSeek V4.1 Flash's Automation-Bench score (54.8, ahead of Opus 5) shows the open-weight tier closing the gap on agentic workflow-automation evals specifically, at a fraction of the cost. Worth flagging as a genuinely new axis of competition: "cheapest model that clears an agentic-automation bar" rather than "most capable at any price."

### Coding
Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5 remain the named-flagship leaders on SWE-bench, but DeepSeek V4.1 Flash's DeepSWE v1.1 score (74.2%, edging out Opus 5) is the more interesting story — an MIT-licensed, drastically cheaper open-weight model beating a premium flagship on a coding-agent benchmark. Treat this as narrowing the practical (not just leaderboard) gap for coding-agent workloads specifically.

### Multimodal (vision, voice, video)
DeepSeek V4.1 Flash adds native multimodal (image) input to its architecture family — a real product capability, not a benchmark tweak — making it the most notable open-weight multimodal entrant this cycle. xAI's Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0 remains the voice leader; Gemini 3.8 Flash still leads agentic finance/legal multimodal-adjacent tasks. Gemini's real multimodal upgrade stays gated behind the still-unshipped 3.5 Pro.

### Long Context & Memory
Meta Muse Spark 1.3 (1M, 98.5% retrieval) and Kimi K3 (1M) still lead; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash also ships at 1M. Gemini's rumored 2M window remains unshipped. No movement this cycle.

### Cost & Efficiency
DeepSeek V4.1 Flash resets the cost-efficiency floor this cycle: MIT-licensed, beats named flagships on several agentic benchmarks, and prices at $0.15-0.30/M tokens (with $0.003/M off-peak cache hits) — roughly 20-50x cheaper than a Fable 5.1 or Astra API call. The one real caveat is verbosity (higher output-token counts than peers can eat into headline savings). Gemini 3.8 Flash and GLM-5.3 remain credible mid-tier efficiency plays but are no longer the sharpest data point in this category. At the macro level, hyperscaler capex continues outrunning free cash flow — UBS now projects $4.1T combined AI infrastructure spend 2026-2028 (roughly triple the prior six years), while S&P Global's $1.3T-in-2027 (5 of 6 hyperscalers FCF-negative) figure stands unchanged.

## What This Means for Platform Leaders

- **Open-weight just got materially cheaper without giving up much capability.** DeepSeek V4.1 Flash beating named flagships on several agentic/coding benchmarks at 20-50x lower price, under an unrestricted MIT license, is the strongest "benchmark your default frontier-API call against an open-weight alternative" signal tracked yet — verify the verbosity caveat (higher output-token counts) before assuming the headline savings hold in production.
- **Cross-lab benchmark and IP disputes are now a real vendor-risk factor, not just a curiosity.** Anthropic's accusation that Moonshot secretly routed Kimi traffic to Claude means any evaluation you've run against Kimi K3 during the affected window may not reflect Kimi's actual capability — treat unverified claims from labs currently in a public dispute with extra scrutiny, in either direction.
- **Chain-of-thought monitorability remains a live procurement question.** OpenAI's Sept 9 system-card update narrowed rather than resolved its own disclosure — its metagaming-detection claims now explicitly cover only what's visible in CoT. If you rely on inspecting model reasoning for compliance or safety review, this narrowing is the material fact, not the reassurance framing around it.
- **Treat every remaining "coming soon" (Gemini 3.5 Pro, Grok 4.7/4.8, Mistral's flagship) as indefinite — and don't assume "shelved" beats "delayed."** xAI's move from delaying Grok 4.7 to skipping it entirely for a bigger, even-less-ready Grok 4.8 is a worse signal than another slipped date; it suggests the org is chasing parameter count over shippability.
- **Anthropic's IPO case is now built on company-sourced numbers, not just investor rumor — read the "profitable" claim the way you'd read any adjusted-EBITDA figure from a pre-IPO company burning heavily on compute.** Nasdaq is locked in; the real test is whether the eventual S-1 uses the same adjustments as the investor-call framing.
- **Hyperscaler capex continues compounding ahead of cash flow** (UBS: $4.1T combined 2026-2028, triple the prior six years) — plan pricing and capacity assumptions on continued compute abundance, but keep watching the assumed 2028 FCF inflection as the single number this entire model depends on.
- **Consider the sudden ad-monetization move inside ChatGPT (sponsored agents, Sept 16) a leading indicator for how labs will fund the compute buildout beyond subscriptions/API** — if you're integrating a consumer-facing assistant into a workflow, start asking vendors now how sponsored content will be disclosed and whether it can bias retrieval or recommendations.
