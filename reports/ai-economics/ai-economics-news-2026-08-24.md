# AI Economics News Report — 2026-08-24

## Executive Summary

This cycle's headline is Goldman Sachs's first cross-country labor-market study (2026-08-19), and it's the most concrete evidence yet that AI's employment effect is real, measurable, and geographically uneven. Analyzing job-openings growth across 800+ occupations in multiple developed economies, Goldman found call-center employment now running 39% below trend in the US, 33% below trend in Canada, and 27% below trend in Germany — with software publishing, management consulting, and advertising showing the same pattern. The effect concentrates hardest on entry-level workers, and the country spread (US > Canada > Germany) is itself a data point: it suggests labor-market institutions and AI adoption speed both matter for how the shock actually lands, not just occupational exposure. This directly extends (not repeats) the June "AI Job Apocalypse" report and April's "16,000 jobs/month" estimate this report has tracked — it's the first version with real cross-country variation attached.

Central-bank systemic-risk warnings widened from a US/UK story into a genuinely global one. Five ECB economists published a Bank blog (2026-08-17) arguing a market correction in AI-linked equities is "likely" based on historical technological-revolution research — regardless of whether current valuations turn out to be fundamentally justified — and flagged €440 billion of European household exposure to the "Magnificent Seven." That's the ECB joining the Fed, Bank of England, and IMF in this report's systemic-risk chorus, but with a distinct and more theoretically grounded argument: corrections happen even when the technology is real, because investor overconfidence pushes prices past fundamentals during every prior general-purpose-technology cycle. Separately, a Bank Underground research note co-authored with IMF chief economist Silvana Tenreyro makes a genuinely novel and underappreciated claim: AI's productivity gains may not lower inflation the way textbook models assume, because investment and consumer spending race ahead of realized productivity gains, and the resulting supply crunches (chip prices, service-sector wage spillovers) can push prices up during the transition rather than down. On financing, the OpenAI-Nvidia Ohio data-center deal was repriced from initial reports of ~$250B down to a signed $105B — a swing large enough to reignite "circular financing" concerns (chip makers financing the infrastructure that buys their own chips) that hit Nvidia shares intraday. Finally, geographic coverage improves this cycle with hard reporting from China: state media and Western outlets both describe accelerating white-collar AI displacement (programmers, scriptwriters) even as youth unemployment sits near 15.6% and the government leans into AI/robotics industrial policy despite the employment tension it creates.

## Goldman Sachs: AI Is Measurably Slowing Job-Openings Growth Across Developed Economies — Call Centers Down 39% (US), 33% (Canada), 27% (Germany) vs. Trend

`labor` `displacement` `data` `us` `global` `investment-research` · **Source:** [Goldman Sachs Research, via CNBC — Goldman studied where AI is squeezing labor markets](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/goldman-ai-impact-employment-jobs.html) · *Found: 2026-08-19*

Goldman Sachs (Tier 2) analyzed employment growth across more than 800 occupations in multiple developed economies and found industries with greater AI-automation exposure have shown systematically slower job-openings growth since the second half of 2022 — a relationship "particularly pronounced" in the US, Germany, and Australia. Call-center employment is now 39% below its historical trend in the US, 33% below trend in Canada, and 27% below trend in Germany; software publishing, management consulting, and advertising services show the same pattern. The effect is strongest for entry-level workers specifically, echoing (and now cross-nationally corroborating) this report's prior coverage of Goldman's "16,000 jobs/month" and "9% of workforce, 15M workers over 10 years" displacement estimates. The genuinely new information here is the cross-country spread: the US shows the sharpest deviation, Germany the mildest among the three named economies, which is the first hard evidence in this report's coverage that country-level labor-market institutions and AI-adoption speed shape how the same technological shock actually lands — a useful corrective to treating "AI displacement" as a single US-centric number.

## Five ECB Economists Warn an AI Market Correction Is "Likely" — Citing Historical Tech-Revolution Precedent, Not Current Valuation Metrics

`valuation` `regulation` `global` `contrarian` `report` · **Source:** [CNBC — 'Worrisome': AI is driving a looming market correction, European central bank economists warn](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/18/ai-tech-rally-correction-economists.html) · *Found: 2026-08-17*

Five ECB staff economists (Andersson, Breckenfelder, Corradin, Nikolov, Viola) published a Bank blog post arguing that "economic research on past technological revolutions points to a worrisome conclusion: a correction of current stock market valuations is likely" — and, notably, that a correction should be expected *even if* current AI valuations accurately reflect the technology's real capacity to reshape the economy, because overconfident and overoptimistic investors reliably push prices past fundamental value during every prior general-purpose-technology diffusion cycle (railroads, electricity, the internet). This is the ECB (Tier 1 central bank) joining the Fed's systemic-risk commentary, the Bank of England's quantified -2.2% UK GDP burst scenario, and the IMF's GFSR concentration warning — but with the most theoretically distinct argument of the four: not "valuations look stretched by metric X" but "the historical base rate for this kind of technology cycle is a correction, independent of whether the technology delivers." The ECB also quantified Europe's specific exposure: European households hold roughly €440 billion in "Magnificent Seven" stock exposure, meaning a US correction is a direct EU financial-stability question, not just a market-sentiment one.

## Bank Underground/IMF: AI's Productivity Gains May Not Curb Inflation — the Investment-Payoff Timing Lag Is Itself Inflationary

`productivity` `gdp` `academic` `contrarian` `global` · **Source:** [Bank Underground, co-authored with IMF Chief Economist Silvana Tenreyro — via Manila Times summary](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/08/22/business/top-business/imf-official-ai-productivity-gains-may-not-curb-inflation/2410148) · *Found: 2026-08-22*

A Bank of England "Bank Underground" research note co-authored by IMF Chief Economist Silvana Tenreyro (Tier 1), Bank of England economist Jenny Chan, and doctoral researcher Ludovica Ambrosino makes a mechanism-level argument that inverts the standard "productivity growth is disinflationary" intuition for the AI transition specifically: business investment and household spending both tend to move ahead of *realized* productivity gains, creating supply crunches that push prices up rather than down during the buildout phase. Specific channels: AI-driven data-center demand pushes up computer-chip prices (raising consumer electronics costs broadly); and productivity gains concentrated in export-oriented sectors raise domestic wages and demand for services, which face their own supply constraints and so raise service-sector inflation. Only productivity gains realized directly in the service sector reliably lower domestic inflation in their framework. No numbers or point forecasts are attached — this is a mechanism paper, not a calibrated model — but it's a genuinely novel angle this report hasn't tracked before: most of the "AI and inflation" commentary to date has been "productivity growth should eventually help the Fed's inflation fight"; this argues the *timing mismatch* between AI investment and AI payoff could do the opposite in the interim.

## OpenAI-Nvidia's $105B Ohio Data-Center Deal Lands $145B Below Initial Reports — Reigniting "Circular Financing" Concerns

`capex` `valuation` `contrarian` `investment-research` `us` · **Source:** [Fortune — OpenAI data center deal with Nvidia comes in $145 billion lower than reported](https://fortune.com/2026/08/18/openai-data-center-deal-with-nvidia-comes-in-145-billion-lower-than-reportedsignaling-concerns-of-artificial-demand-for-chips/) · *Found: 2026-08-18*

Nvidia signed a deal (2026-08-17) guaranteeing $105 billion in lease and power obligations for OpenAI's new Ohio data center — an 8-10 gigawatt facility built and managed by SB Energy under a 20-year lease, with Nvidia as exclusive chip supplier and a separate $1.5B direct investment in SB Energy. The number matters because of the trajectory: initial reports put the guarantee near $250B, a subsequent report cut it to under $120B (2026-08-14), and the final signed figure landed at $105B — a $145B reduction from the earliest reporting. That repricing path is exactly the kind of data point that feeds the "circular financing" critique this report has tracked since SemiAnalysis's value-capture analysis last cycle: Nvidia — the chip supplier — is directly financing the infrastructure that generates demand for its own chips, raising the question of whether reported "demand" reflects end-customer revenue or vendor-financed capacity. Nvidia shares fell roughly 4.5% intraday on the larger guarantee figures before the final number settled lower. Treat the underlying capex/financing numbers as hard data (SEC-adjacent disclosure); treat "artificial demand" as a contested interpretation, not a settled fact — but the deal's steep markdown from initial reporting is itself notable regardless of which interpretation is correct.

## China: AI-Driven White-Collar Layoffs Accelerate Even as State Doubles Down on AI/Robotics Industrial Policy

`labor` `displacement` `china` `global` `report` · **Source:** [The Washington Post — Workers in China worry over being replaced as they adapt to the growing impact of AI on jobs](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/08/23/china-ai-jobs-unemployment-youth/f9ddb16c-9f6d-11f1-8606-1d40ad00172e_story.html) · *Found: 2026-08-23*

Wire reporting (also carried by AP/ABC News, BNN Bloomberg, and Manila Times) documents accelerating AI-driven displacement across Chinese white-collar occupations — programmers, scriptwriters, and other codifiable knowledge work — against a backdrop of state industrial policy that explicitly prioritizes AI and robotics competitiveness with the US over near-term employment protection. One named case: Beijing programmer Fei Zhaojun was laid off along with roughly 160 colleagues after his employer concluded AI could replace the team's coding work. China's youth (16-24) unemployment remains elevated near 15.6% even as AI-adjacent hiring grows sharply (AI-engineer postings +31% YoY, robotics-algorithm-engineer postings +57% YoY per other outlets this month) — the same "aggregate job creation coexists with concentrated, painful displacement" pattern this report has documented in US Challenger/Fed data, but here it is layered onto a government that, per the article, is willing to accept employment friction as the cost of AI/robotics leadership. This is a genuine fill of this report's longstanding non-US coverage gap, though it should be read as quality journalism (Tier 3), not a primary statistical release — no new official Chinese labor statistic accompanies it this cycle.

## Market Commentary: AI-Bubble Valuation Metrics Hit Historical Extremes — Shiller CAPE >41, Buffett Indicator ~238%

`valuation` `contrarian` `data` `us` · **Source:** [The Motley Fool — If an AI Bubble Burst Is Coming, Warren Buffett's 1999 Warning Is More Crucial Than Ever](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/24/if-an-ai-bubble-burst-is-coming-warren-buffetts-19/) · *Found: 2026-08-24*

As of 2026-08-24, the S&P 500's Shiller CAPE ratio stands above 41 — its second-highest reading in the index's history — while the "Buffett Indicator" (total US market cap to GDP) sits near a record 238%, well past the ~200% level Buffett himself flagged as "playing with fire" in 2001. Market concentration has also reached a new extreme: the top 10 S&P 500 stocks now represent roughly 35% of the index, versus 25% at the dot-com bubble's 2000 peak. This is Tier 4 commentary (a financial-media piece, not primary research), but the underlying valuation ratios are real, widely-tracked data points, and they land the same week as the ECB's correction warning and the ongoing hyperscaler-bond-demand softening this report has tracked since last cycle — three independent signals (a central bank's historical-base-rate argument, a market-plumbing indicator, and a headline valuation-ratio extreme) now pointing the same direction, even though none of them individually proves a bubble is about to pop. Countervailing point retained from prior coverage: unlike 1999-2000, today's largest AI-exposed companies (Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon) generate genuine profits in the hundreds of billions and sell to paying enterprise customers, not just speculative retail flow.
