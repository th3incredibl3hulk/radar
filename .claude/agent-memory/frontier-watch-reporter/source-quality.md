From the 2026-07-11 (first) report, reconfirmed 2026-07-31:

**High value:**
- Direct WebSearch queries per-lab ("<lab> new model release <month year>") surfaced primary lab blog posts (anthropic.com/news, x.ai/news, ai.meta.com/blog, mistral.ai/news, blog.google, newsroom.amd.com) reliably and quickly.
- TechCrunch, CNBC, Bloomberg, and AMD/company newsroom press releases consistently had the clearest first-party-adjacent reporting on release timing, pricing, and deal terms (e.g. the AMD-Anthropic deal was best sourced directly from newsroom.amd.com).
- llm-stats.com, BenchLM.ai, and swebench.com are reliable benchmark-aggregator hits for "who's ahead" data (SWE-bench Verified/Pro/Lite splits) — check these directly each cycle.
- Digitalapplied.com's "wave tracker" style posts (e.g. the July 2026 open-weight wave roundup) were unusually good at synthesizing a multi-lab story (Kimi K3 + Inkling + Mistral + MiniMax) in one search hit — worth searching for "<topic> wave" or "<topic> tracker" style queries when multiple labs move at once.

**Lower value / caution:**
- Low-quality SEO aggregators (skycrumbs.com, coursiv.io, releasebot.io) sometimes synthesize claims (e.g. a "DeepMind prospective credit assignment paper") that could not be verified against any primary source on follow-up search — treat single-aggregator-sourced technical/research claims as unconfirmed until a primary source (arxiv.org, deepmind.google) is found. Dropped one such claim entirely in the 2026-07-31 report rather than report it with a hedge.
- Still did not get direct hits on newsletter-tier sources (Import AI, Interconnects, The Batch, SemiAnalysis, AInews/smol.ai) via generic WebSearch two cycles in a row — consider WebFetch-ing their homepages/archives directly next run instead of relying on search snippets to surface them.
- When a last report is >2 weeks old (as it was this cycle: 20 days), a strict "most recent 2 weeks only" window creates a coverage gap (here, 07-11 to 07-17) — resolved by backfilling only stories foundational to an in-window follow-on (Inkling/Kimi K3 origin dates, since their public-weights release was in-window). Worth deciding proactively next time a gap appears rather than re-deriving the judgment call.

**Update 2026-08-17:** Newsletter tier still not directly reachable — Interconnects WebFetch returned 403; generic search on Interconnects/SemiAnalysis surfaces about-pages and old posts, not current issues. SemiAnalysis's own newsletter.semianalysis.com URLs do surface via targeted search ("SemiAnalysis compute capex <month>") even if the Interconnects/Import AI equivalents don't — worth trying lab-specific newsletter URLs directly (newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/...) rather than the marketing homepage. HF Daily Papers checked directly (huggingface.co/papers/trending) this cycle per plan — still no landmark hit, 3 cycles running; likely genuinely a quiet stretch for headline papers rather than a search-tactic failure. Generic WebSearch with specific dates ("<topic> August 14 15 16 2026") worked well to surface fresh multi-story roundups efficiently — cheaper than one query per story.

**Tactic that worked well:** 5-6 parallel WebSearch calls per batch (one per lab/topic), across 3 batches total (labs → open-weight/capex/papers/benchmarks → compute deals/gov't status follow-ups) — converged on all major stories for a ~$1.5 total budget spend on a $2 cap. Papers/research searches remain the weakest link — two cycles running, generic "landmark paper" searches surface either nothing citable or low-quality aggregator synthesis; consider going directly to arxiv.org listing pages or Hugging Face Daily Papers next time instead of keyword search.

**Update 2026-09-07:** Confirmed the lesson filed in coverage-log.md this cycle — during a multi-lab release pileup (Sept 1-3: Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, and it turns out Google too), lab-by-lab WebSearch queries must be run for literally every tracked lab each cycle, even ones that seem quiet; Gemini 3.8 Flash was missed on 2026-09-05 by assumption rather than a search-tactic failure. Anthropic's own research/news blog (anthropic.com/research, anthropic.com/news) surfaced the Fermat's Last Theorem story cleanly via a single targeted WebSearch — first-party lab blogs remain the highest-value source when you already know roughly what to search for. Finance/business press (Fortune, PYMNTS, Motley Fool, CNBC) was the right tier for the Anthropic IPO and hyperscaler capex/FCF stories — these are economics-adjacent but strategically material to frontier-watch's "lab strategy" mandate; ai-economics-reporter likely covers the same hyperscaler-FCF story in more depth, worth checking for overlap/complementary framing next time both reports run close together.

**Update 2026-09-17 (double-length 10-day backfill cycle):** The lab-by-lab sweep discipline (one WebSearch per tracked lab: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen, Moonshot, plus Tencent/Z.AI as open-weight checks) paid off directly — it caught DeepSeek's new architecture family (V4.1 Flash) and confirmed Meta/Mistral/Qwen were genuinely quiet rather than missed. WebSearch's own synthesized answers remained strong for fast-moving multi-source stories (Grok 4.7→4.8 timeline, Anthropic-Moonshot dispute, Anthropic IPO) — a single targeted query with a specific date range reliably surfaced a coherent timeline without needing WebFetch. One dedicated WebFetch call (to a Hugging Face blog post) was used to confirm a publish date and decide an item was too old for the window (the ICML reproducibility hackathon, published Aug 13 — correctly excluded rather than force-fit into the report). Aggregator/blog sites (KuCoin news, BigGo Finance, explainx.ai, orcarouter.ai, iweaver.ai) reliably tracked the Grok 4.7/4.8 saga's exact dates when official xAI channels published nothing — useful specifically for "what did xAI actually ship vs. announce" questions where the primary source is silent. Dealroom.co and Cryptopolitan were the best sources for the Anthropic-Moonshot dispute specifics (request counts, account counts) — worth remembering finance/crypto-adjacent news aggregators sometimes cover AI-lab disputes with more numeric detail than mainstream tech press. Lesson reinforced: when a "who's ahead" prediction depends on an index score (AA Intelligence Index), always check for a version bump before citing a number — v4.2 and v4.3 landed only 3 days apart this cycle, and the underlying scale is not comparable across versions (a score of "53" post-v4.3 is not directly comparable to "65.7" pre-v4.2).

**Update 2026-09-05:** WebSearch's own synthesized summaries (not just link snippets) were unusually rich this cycle — for major releases (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1/Mythos 5.1, Muse Spark 1.3, GLM-5.3) a single well-targeted query returned benchmark tables, pricing, and dates good enough to write the report entry directly, without needing the article-summarizer subagent or WebFetch at all. Given a tight $2 budget, skipping the summarizer-dispatch step (called for in the shared template) and relying on WebSearch synthesis directly was the right tradeoff — it covered 10 solid entries + full SOTA update + memory refresh for ~$1.6 total. Reserve the summarizer/WebFetch path for when WebSearch snippets are thin or conflicting, not as a default step, when operating under a tight budget. Landmark-paper search is now a confirmed 4-cycle pattern of genuine quiet, not a search-tactic failure — deprioritize deep paper search under budget pressure unless a newsletter/aggregator flags something specific. AA Index leaderboard changes fastest right after major releases (2 new entrants — Astra, Muse Spark 1.3 — reshuffled the whole top 8 within days) — always re-pull the live leaderboard rather than trusting the prior snapshot's ranks when 2+ new flagships shipped in-window.
